What I build

Common SME problems, built as apps.

Pick the one that's hurting most. Start there. Add more as the business grows into them — each app on the same platform, sharing the same data.

Inventory & stock
An inventory system that actually matches what's on the shelf. Barcode or QR scan, multi-location, reorder alerts. Replace the spreadsheet that's permanently out of date.
Job & production tracking
See every order from intake to delivery. Know what's done, what's late, what's blocking. Production tracking for small business — without an MES.

Why this, not the alternatives

Cheaper than a developer. Lighter than an ERP. Fits your workflow.

Three honest comparisons — written so you can decide whether this is the right shape for your problem, before you spend a cent.

01 · vs hiring a developer

Cheaper and faster.

No engineering team to manage, no months of estimation, no surprise change requests. Most apps are live in a few weeks, for a fraction of what a custom build costs. When the business changes, you change the app — not a sprint backlog.

02 · vs an ERP

Lighter and more flexible.

No six-figure licence, no eighteen-month rollout, no department forced to change how it works. Start with the one part that's hurting. An affordable ERP alternative for small business — built only where you actually need it.

03 · vs off-the-shelf SaaS

Actually fits your workflow.

Generic SaaS makes you bend to its forms. A custom app bends to yours. The fields are the fields you already use. The flow is the flow you already run. Your operation isn't shaped like a stranger's product — it shouldn't have to be.

Where it fits — and where it doesn't

Honest about no-code.

No-code is the right shape for operational apps: tracking, forms, approvals, workflows, dashboards, mobile-first data capture, sub-thousand-user internal tools. It is not the right shape for high-volume consumer apps, real-time control systems, complex algorithmic logic, or anything that needs millisecond latency. If your problem is on the wrong side of that line, I will tell you on the call — and point you to where it does belong.

FAQ

Buyer questions, answered directly.

Most founders ask me the same eight things on the first call. The short answers are below — the long ones, you get on the call.

How do I get a custom app for my business without hiring a developer?

Work with a no-code app developer. The app is configured on a no-code platform — in our case, Jodoo — so it can be built in weeks instead of months, costs a fraction of a developer team, and you own the platform when it's done.

You name one specific problem (inventory, job tracking, quotations, approvals, CRM, maintenance, field reports). You get a fixed-scope, fixed-fee proposal after a 30-minute discovery call. You go live in two to six weeks.

What's a cheaper alternative to an ERP for a small company?

For most SMEs in Singapore, Malaysia and Brunei, a custom no-code app is a far cheaper alternative to an ERP. You skip the six-figure licence, the long rollout, and the consultant hours.

Start with the one part of the business that's hurting — inventory, production tracking, quotations, CRM — build a small app that fits exactly how you work, and add more modules only when you need them. No big-bang go-live, no department forced to change its workflow.

How do I move my business off spreadsheets?

Start with the one spreadsheet that costs you the most — usually inventory, jobs, or quotes. Replace it with a small custom app on a no-code platform.

The fields stay the fields your team already uses; the difference is that the data is now structured, shared, audited, and accessible on a phone. Once that's running, replace the next spreadsheet, then the next. Iterate, not big-bang.

What is a no-code app platform, and is it safe to run a business on?

A no-code app platform lets you build business apps by configuration instead of writing code. Velo Working builds on Jodoo — a no-code app platform used by tens of thousands of companies across Asia, with database, workflow, audit trail, permissions, API and reporting built in.

The platform stays in your company name, the data is yours, and your own staff can keep editing the app after I hand it over.

How long does a custom business app take to build?

A single focused app — inventory system, job tracking, quotations, approvals, CRM, maintenance, project tracking, field reports — typically takes two to six weeks from kick-off to go-live, depending on data migration and how many users we onboard.

Larger scope is broken into phases so you see working software every two weeks. No nine-month dark tunnels.

Will I be locked in to your service?

No. The platform account is in your company name, the data is yours, the documentation lives in your account, and the app runs without me.

You can keep changing it yourself, hire someone else to maintain it, or just leave it as is. I stay reachable for task-based work — you pay only when there's actually work to do.

Which countries do you serve?

Velo Working serves SMEs across Singapore, Malaysia and Brunei. The discovery call is on WhatsApp or video — physical site visits are charged separately if needed.

What's a discovery call, and is there a charge?

A thirty-minute call where you describe the problem and I ask questions. By the end you have a written scope, a fixed quote, and a delivery date.

There's no charge and no obligation. If your problem isn't a fit for a no-code app, I'll tell you on the call and point you to where it does belong.
