Velo Working / Field notes / Positioning Jodoo

Three framings.
One battlefield.

Cloud ERP, Low-Code, No-Code — the same three categories, plotted three different ways. Each framing tells a different story to a different buyer. Only one survives the long-term test.

Field notes Positioning May 2026 ~6 min read

When an SME owner asks "should we go for SAP, build it ourselves on a low-code platform, or run it on Jodoo?" — the honest answer depends on which axes you choose to compare them on.

We worked through three framings before we settled on how to talk about Jodoo on the rest of this site. We're publishing the full comparison because the choice itself is instructive: positioning isn't about which category wins on paper. It's about which axis matters once the system is live.

Three candidate framings
A · Economics  ·  B · Organisational  ·  C · Battlefield
Framing A Economics

3-year TCO × Time to adapt

X-axis: Total cost of ownership (3 years)
Y-axis: Time to adapt to a process change

"ERP costs millions and takes months to change. No-Code costs thousands and changes in hours."

Buyer
CFO — money in, money out.
Strength
Universal recognition. Numbers speak.
Risk
Frames Jodoo as the "cheap" option — risks looking lightweight.
Framing B Organisational

Resource demand × Self-sufficiency

X-axis: Resource demand (capital + people + time)
Y-axis: Operational self-sufficiency

"ERP needs an IT department. Low-Code needs developers. No-Code needs trained operations staff."

Buyer
MD & board — org structure and hiring plan.
Strength
Maps to "do we need an IT team?" — an answer the SME already knows.
Risk
Less immediate than money. Buyer has to think one step.
Framing C Chosen

Cost to evolve × Operational autonomy

X-axis: Cost of every change after deployment
Y-axis: Operational autonomy (no outside help needed)

"Implementation is one-time. Evolution is forever. Only No-Code keeps you free."

Buyer
Owner-operator — long-term cost of dependency.
Strength
Renders the whole thesis visually. Both axes describe life after deployment.
Bonus
Doesn't undermine implementation revenue — both axes are post-deployment.
Why Framing C wins

Both axes describe the life after the system goes live.

Framings A and B both place No-Code in the right quadrant — but they describe what's true before the system goes live. Once the category is bought and deployed, its cost-to-buy stops mattering. What matters is the rent the system charges for every subsequent change.

Framing C is the only one of the three where both axes describe the life of the system after the implementation partner leaves the room. That's the actual moat — and the only framing that doesn't accidentally reduce the implementation work to "the cheap option."

Framing A · Economics
Strong but limiting

Frames Jodoo as the budget choice. Good for cost-conscious buyers, weak for premium positioning.

Framing B · Organisational
Smart but indirect

Requires the buyer to translate "needs IT department" into "we don't have one." Slower in transactional rooms.

Framing C · Battlefield
Both axes favour No-Code

No tradeoff for the buyer to weigh. ERP sits in the worst quadrant, No-Code alone in the best — over the entire life of the system.

What it means in practice

Three implications for SMEs choosing an operations system.

01

Compare on the change, not the launch.

The first deployment is roughly the same headache for any of the three categories. The difference is what each one costs to alter on day 200, day 800, day 2,000.

02

Autonomy is a balance-sheet item.

If every form change requires a vendor invoice, you've turned an operations system into a permanent rent. Operational autonomy compounds — quietly — on every change you make in-house.

03

Implementation work isn't the discount.

The right framing distinguishes "the platform you bought" from "the system you'll evolve." Real partners earn fees on the build and hand the keys over for the long, ongoing evolution.
